# Tailoring Messages to Audiences

The same update lands very differently with an executive, a delivery team member, and a
customer. Tailoring means changing the *content, depth, framing, and channel* per audience while
keeping the underlying facts consistent. Mismatched messaging is the most common avoidable
communication failure: execs buried in detail tune out; teams given only headlines cannot act.

## Three core audiences (Helios)

### Executives / sponsor (e.g. VP of Support, Finance)
- **They care about:** outcomes, risks to those outcomes, decisions they must make, money, dates.
- **Depth:** high-level. Lead with the bottom line (RAG, on/off track, the ask). One page max.
- **Framing:** business impact, not technical cause. "Launch at risk; need a decision on scope by
  Friday" — not "the SSO token refresh is flaky".
- **Channel:** steering deck, exec summary, short standing meeting. Push, then a forum to decide.

### Delivery team (engineers, designers, QA)
- **They care about:** what to do next, dependencies, blockers, decisions that change their work.
- **Depth:** detailed and specific. Technical context is welcome and necessary.
- **Framing:** concrete and actionable — tasks, owners, dates, acceptance criteria.
- **Channel:** standup, board/dashboard, chat, tickets. Interactive and pull-friendly.

### Customers / end users (beta customers, call-centre agents)
- **They care about:** what changes for them, when, and what they must do.
- **Depth:** plain-language, benefit-led; no internal jargon or project mechanics.
- **Framing:** impact and timing. "From 3 March you can reset your password without calling."
- **Channel:** release notes, email, in-app notice, training session. Mostly push, with a
  feedback route.

## Communication modes (how information moves)

| Mode | What it is | Good for | Helios example |
|------|------------|----------|----------------|
| **Push** | Sender delivers to recipients | Broad, one-way updates | Email digest, status report, release notes |
| **Pull** | Recipient fetches when they want | Reference info, large/optional detail | Dashboard, wiki, shared drive |
| **Interactive** | Real-time, two-way exchange | Decisions, alignment, conflict, nuance | Steering meeting, demo, workshop, standup |

Rule of thumb: the more important the *shared understanding* (decisions, conflict, sensitive
news), the more interactive the mode should be. Reserve push for routine information and pull for
the long tail of detail nobody needs on a schedule.

## Tailoring checklist
- Could this audience act on the message as written? (right depth)
- Does the framing match what *they* care about, not what *you* find interesting?
- Is the channel one they actually monitor?
- Is there a way for them to respond? (every audience needs a feedback path)
