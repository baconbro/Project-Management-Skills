# Stakeholder Analysis Techniques

Three complementary lenses for analyzing stakeholders. Start with the power/interest grid;
reach for the salience model and the cube when the grid is too coarse to decide.

## 1. Power / Interest Grid

Plot each stakeholder on two axes — **power** (ability to affect the project's outcome,
budget, or continuation) and **interest** (how much the outcome affects them). Each axis is
rated High or Low (or H/M/L for finer resolution). The quadrant dictates the default strategy.

```
            HIGH POWER
               |
  Keep         |   Manage
  Satisfied    |   Closely
               |
  -------------+-------------  HIGH INTEREST
               |
  Monitor      |   Keep
  (min effort) |   Informed
               |
            LOW POWER
```

- **Manage Closely** (high power, high interest): your sponsor and key users. Engage fully,
  involve in decisions, never surprise them. For Helios this is the VP of Support.
- **Keep Satisfied** (high power, low interest): security/compliance, finance. Give them what
  they need without over-loading; their interest spikes the moment something goes wrong.
- **Keep Informed** (low power, high interest): call-centre agents, beta customers. Keep them
  in the loop; they are advocates and a source of ground truth.
- **Monitor** (low power, low interest): peripheral teams. Minimal, periodic contact.

Re-plot whenever the project changes — a Monitor stakeholder can jump to Manage Closely when
their area becomes a dependency.

## 2. Salience Model (Mitchell, Agle & Wood)

When power and interest alone do not separate stakeholders, classify by three attributes:

- **Power** — can impose their will.
- **Legitimacy** — has a rightful, appropriate claim.
- **Urgency** — needs attention now (time-sensitive and important).

Overlaps define stakeholder types:

| Attributes present        | Type        | Treatment                                  |
|---------------------------|-------------|--------------------------------------------|
| One only                  | Latent      | Low priority, monitor                      |
| Two                       | Expectant   | Active engagement                          |
| All three                 | Definitive  | Top priority — act immediately             |
| Power + Urgency, no legit | Dangerous   | Manage carefully; can coerce               |

A regulator with a looming deadline (legitimacy + urgency, gaining power) is a definitive
stakeholder you cannot defer.

## 3. Stakeholder Cube

Extends the 2D grid into three dimensions to plan tailored tactics:

- **Power** (high / low) — as above.
- **Interest / Attitude** (supportive / opposed) — which way they lean.
- **Influence / Dynamism** (active / passive) — how readily their position shifts.

The cube prevents the classic error of treating a high-power supporter and a high-power
opponent identically. A passive opponent needs persuasion; an active opponent needs
containment and a counter-narrative.

## Choosing a technique
- Quick triage and most projects: **power/interest grid**.
- Competing claims, ethics, or "who really counts": **salience model**.
- Planning differentiated influence tactics: **stakeholder cube**.
