# Adoption and Readiness

Delivering a system or process is only half the job; the other half is getting people to actually
use it. Adoption (organizational change management) is the discipline of moving people from the
current way of working to the new one. A technically perfect Helios portal that call-centre
agents quietly ignore — still phoning customers the old way — is a failed project.

## ADKAR: the individual change sequence
People change one at a time, in order. ADKAR names the five steps each person must complete; an
adoption gap is usually a *specific* missing step, which tells you the fix.

| Step | The person... | Gap symptom | Lever |
|------|---------------|-------------|-------|
| **A — Awareness** | knows *why* the change is happening | "Why are we doing this?" | Communicate the case for change |
| **D — Desire** | *wants* to participate and support it | "What's in it for me? I'd rather not." | Address WIIFM, involve, surface concerns |
| **K — Knowledge** | knows *how* to change | "I don't know how to use it." | Training, documentation, job aids |
| **A — Ability** | *can* do it in practice | "I learned it but I'm slow / it's hard." | Coaching, practice, support, time |
| **R — Reinforcement** | *sustains* the new behaviour | "I've slipped back to the old way." | Metrics, recognition, remove the old path |

Diagnose at the weakest step: training (Knowledge) does nothing if the real blocker is Desire.

## Readiness assessment
Before launch, gauge how ready each stakeholder group is to adopt:
- **Impact:** how much does their day-to-day change? (bigger change = more support needed)
- **Attitude:** supportive / neutral / resistant today? (pull current stance from `stakeholder-management`)
- **Capacity:** do they have the time, skills, and tools to absorb the change?
- **History:** how have past changes gone here? (change fatigue is real)
Map groups by impact and attitude; high-impact, resistant groups (the Helios agents) get the most
deliberate plan.

## Resistance management
Resistance is information, not an obstacle to crush. Treat it as a signal:
- **Find the root cause:** fear of job loss, extra workload, lost status, a genuine flaw, or
  simply not being consulted.
- **Respond to the cause, not the symptom:** involvement and early input prevent more resistance
  than any amount of persuasion after the fact.
- **Engage influencers:** a respected sceptic who turns becomes your best advocate.
- **Distinguish** "this is badly designed" (fix the product) from "I don't want to change" (work
  the ADKAR steps). The first kind of resistance is a gift.

## Reinforcement: making it stick
Adoption decays without reinforcement. After go-live:
- **Measure** adoption (usage, ticket-deflection rate) and act on what the numbers show.
- **Recognize and celebrate** early adopters and wins.
- **Remove the old path** where feasible, so reverting is not an easy option.
- **Close the loop** on feedback — fix the friction users hit, or they will abandon the new way.
Hand the reinforcement activities into `project-delivery` so they are owned, not assumed.
