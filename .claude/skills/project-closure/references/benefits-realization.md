# Benefits realization — make the value stick after delivery

Load this to hand over and track the benefits a project promised. Most benefits are
realized **after** the project ends, so closure must name who tracks them and when. Tie
this back to the `business-case`, which is where the benefits were originally promised.
Examples use the running sample project: **Helios**, a customer self-service portal.

## Why this matters
A project delivers an output (a portal); the organization wants an outcome (fewer support
calls) and ultimately a benefit (lower cost, happier customers). If no one owns the
benefit after go-live, the project can "succeed" while the investment never pays back.

## From output to benefit
- **Output** — what the project produced. _e.g. the Helios self-service portal is live._
- **Outcome** — the change in behavior. _e.g. customers self-serve routine requests._
- **Benefit** — the measurable value. _e.g. 30% fewer routine support tickets; cost saved._
- **Disbenefit** — any negative consequence to manage. _e.g. a short-term spike in support
  questions during migration._

## The benefits handover (at closure)
1. List each benefit from the `business-case` with its target, its measure, and its
   baseline (the "before" number).
2. Name a single **benefits owner** — usually in operations or the business, not the
   project team — accountable for each benefit after closure.
3. Agree **when** each benefit is measured and reviewed (e.g. 3 and 6 months post-launch).
4. Hand over the measurement method and any tooling so the owner can actually track it.
5. Record all of this in the closure report (see `closure-report.md`).

## Tracking after closure
- Measure actual vs. target on the agreed dates; explain any gap.
- Keep tracking until benefits are realized or formally written off — not just to go-live.
- Feed surprises (benefits over/under target, unforeseen disbenefits) into
  `lessons-learned` so future business cases are more honest.

## A simple benefits table
| Benefit | Measure | Baseline | Target | Owner | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lower support cost | Routine tickets/month | 5,000 | 3,500 | Support lead | +3 mo, +6 mo |
| Faster resolution | Avg. handle time | 12 min | 8 min | Support lead | +6 mo |

## Anti-patterns
- Treating go-live as the finish line and never measuring whether benefits arrived.
- Leaving benefits owned by the project team, who disband at closure.
- Targets with no baseline, so "improvement" cannot be proven.
- Ignoring disbenefits until they become complaints.
