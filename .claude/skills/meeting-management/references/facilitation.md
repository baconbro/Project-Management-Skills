# Facilitation

Facilitation is the discipline of helping a group reach its outcome efficiently. The
facilitator owns the process; the group owns the content.

## Roles

- **Facilitator** — runs the agenda, keeps time, draws out quiet voices, curbs dominant
  ones, and stays neutral on content. On a small team this is often the PM, but a neutral
  facilitator is better for charged topics (e.g. a retro).
- **Scribe / note-taker** — captures decisions and actions in real time so the facilitator
  can stay present. Rotate this role; do not let it always fall on the same person.
- **Timekeeper** — calls timeboxes. Can be the facilitator, but a separate timekeeper
  protects the facilitator from being the "bad guy" alone.
- **Decider** — for decision meetings, name who decides before the discussion starts.

## Timeboxing

- Put a duration on every agenda item and start and end on time. Respecting the clock
  respects everyone in the room.
- When an item runs over, the group chooses explicitly: extend (and cut something else),
  park it, or take it offline. The default is to park it, not to silently overrun.
- Short meetings force focus. A 25-minute meeting is usually a better 30-minute meeting.

## The parking lot

- Keep a visible "parking lot" for important but off-topic items. Capturing them lets you
  move on without anyone feeling dismissed.
- Review the parking lot before close: assign each item an owner and a next step (a later
  meeting, an action item, or "drop it"). An unreviewed parking lot is just a graveyard.

## Decision methods

Choose the method before debating, and say which one you are using:

| Method | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| **Decider decides** (after input) | A clear owner exists and speed matters |
| **Consent** ("any objections?") | You want a workable decision fast, not perfect buy-in |
| **Consensus** | The decision needs everyone's genuine support to stick |
| **Majority vote** | The group is split and a tie-break is needed |

Consensus is the most expensive; reserve it for the few decisions that truly need it.
Default to "decider decides after input" for most project choices.

## Meeting types in brief

- **Kickoff** — align on purpose, scope, roles, and ways of working. Heavy on shared
  understanding, light on decisions.
- **Stand-up** — 15 minutes, status and blockers only; problem-solving goes offline.
- **Steering committee** — decisions and escalations for sponsors; lead with the asks.
- **Review / demo** — show working output and gather feedback against acceptance criteria.
- **Retrospective** — improve how the team works; see `lessons-learned` for formats.
